Student Awareness Toolkit

Recruitment Safety Guide & Red Flag Playbook

Essential knowledge for college students and fresh graduates to recognize fraudulent hiring patterns before sharing sensitive personal data or hard-earned money.

The Absolute Golden Rule of Hiring

Genuine employers pay you; they NEVER ask you to pay them. No legitimate company will ever demand a "registration fee," "training fee," "laptop security deposit," or "document verification fee" under any circumstances. Any request for money via UPI, QR code, or bank transfer is a definitive red flag.

Anatomy of Top 5 Recruitment Traps

1. The "Refundable Training Fee" Trap
Critical Risk

How it works: The applicant receives an offer letter congratulating them on selection, but is told they must pay ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 for training modules, certificates, or asset insurance, promised to be refunded with the first stipend.

Reality Check: Once the transfer is sent, the recruiter stops answering or demands additional "GST clearance" or "server access" fees.
2. The "Telegram / WhatsApp Recruiter"
High Risk

How it works: The recruiter claims to represent top MNCs (Amazon, Google, TCS, Flipkart) but contacts you exclusively via WhatsApp or Telegram using a generic name and a public @gmail.com email.

Reality Check: Legitimate corporations have secure applicant tracking systems (ATS) and correspond exclusively from corporate domain emails (e.g. @amazon.jobs).
3. Vague High-Pay Data Entry / Typing
Common Trap

How it works: Promises of ₹40,000/month for "simple copy paste" or "form filling 2 hours daily" with "zero skills required".

Reality Check: These operations either collect upfront "software demo charges" or enforce impossible accuracy quotas to withhold payment and demand penalty fees.
4. Instant Selection Without Screening
Suspicious

How it works: You receive an official-looking appointment letter within hours of submitting a resume, with no technical interview, screening test, or video call.

Reality Check: Genuine companies invest engineering time in interviewing candidates to gauge culture and technical competence before issuing binding offers.

The JobShield 4-Point Verification Framework

Follow these concrete steps whenever you receive an offer from an unfamiliar recruiter:

Step 1: Check MCA / ROC

Visit mca.gov.in (Master Data) and check if the Corporate Identification Number (CIN) is active and matches the address on the letter.

Step 2: Inspect Email Domain

Examine the sender's domain after the '@'. Ensure it matches the company's real website. Beware of domain spoofs (e.g. @microsoft-careers.com vs @microsoft.com).

Step 3: LinkedIn Recruiter Check

Look up the recruiter on LinkedIn. Verify their tenure, company connections, and whether they actively post company updates.

Step 4: Contact Official Line

Never dial the phone number printed on a suspect offer letter. Look up the company's head office switchboard independently and ask for HR.
